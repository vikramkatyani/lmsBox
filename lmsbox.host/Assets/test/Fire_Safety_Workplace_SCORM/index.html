LESSON 1
Understanding Fire

This lesson covers the fundamental principles of fire and how it behaves. Understanding what fire needs to survive is essential for preventing fires and knowing how to respond in an emergency.

IN THIS LESSON YOU WILL LEARN

  • The three elements required for fire to exist
  • The different classes of fire based on fuel type
  • How fires spread through buildings
LESSON 1
The Triangle of Fire

Fire is a chemical reaction called combustion. For fire to start and survive, three elements must be present.

HEATElectrics, sparks
FUELPaper, wood
OXYGENIn the air
🔥 FIRE
🔥

Heat Sources

Faulty electrical equipment, discarded cigarettes, sparks from machinery

📦

Fuel Sources

Paper, wood, textiles, petrol, escaping gas

💨

Oxygen

Present in the air (approximately 21%)

KEY TAKEAWAY

All three elements – heat, fuel, and oxygen – must be present for fire to exist. Remove any one element and the fire will go out.

LESSON 1
Classes of Fire

Fires are classified based on the type of fuel that is burning. Different classes require different firefighting methods.

A

Ordinary Combustibles

Wood, paper, textiles, plastics

B

Flammable Liquids

Petrol, oils, spirits, grease

C

Flammable Gases

Propane, butane, natural gas

⚡

Electrical

Live equipment, sockets, cables

F

Cooking Oils & Fats

Kitchens, catering areas

KEY TAKEAWAY

Always identify the class of fire before attempting to tackle it – using the wrong method can spread the fire or cause injury.

Fire Prevention

LESSON 2
Preventing Common Fires

Most workplace fires are preventable through simple housekeeping measures and awareness of potential hazards.

EMPTY
Waste bins regularly and dispose of combustible materials like newspapers and oily rags correctly
STORE
Flammable liquids in original containers with lids replaced, away from heat and sunlight
REMOVE
Packaging, cardboard boxes, and combustible materials to safe storage areas promptly
REPORT
Any suspected gas leaks immediately – do not operate electrical switches
KEY TAKEAWAY

Good housekeeping prevents fires – keep your workspace clear of unnecessary combustible materials.

LESSON 2
Electrical Safety

Electrical faults are one of the leading causes of workplace fires.

REPLACE
Electrical leads at the first sign of wear, fraying, or damage
AVOID
Overloading electrical sockets – use one plug per socket where possible
SWITCH OFF
All electrical appliances when not in use, especially overnight
ENSURE
All appliances are PAT tested regularly and air vents are not blocked
REPORT
Any faulty equipment immediately and take it out of service
KEY TAKEAWAY

Never ignore damaged electrical equipment – report it immediately and stop using it.

LESSON 2
Identifying Fire Hazards

Being alert to potential fire hazards is everyone's responsibility.

LOOK
For obstructed fire exits, blocked escape routes, and items near heat sources
CHECK
That fire doors are kept closed and not propped open
NOTICE
Unusual smells such as burning, gas, or overheating equipment
REPORT
Any concerns to your fire warden or H&S representative immediately
KEY TAKEAWAY

If you see a fire hazard, report it – don't assume someone else will.

LESSON 3
Fire Safety Signs

By law, all fire safety signs must include a picture to make them easily recognisable.

🚪

FIRE EXIT

Green with white figure – shows your nearest route out

🔔

FIRE ALARM

Red – shows where to activate the alarm

🧯

EXTINGUISHER

Red – indicates firefighting equipment location

📍

ASSEMBLY POINT

Green – where to gather after evacuating

KEY TAKEAWAY

Know where your nearest fire exit, alarm call point, and assembly point are before you need them.

LESSON 3
Fire Doors and Escape Routes

Fire doors slow the progress of fire and smoke, giving you valuable extra seconds to evacuate.

NEVER
Prop fire doors open with wedges or furniture – they must close automatically
KEEP
Escape routes clear – never store items in corridors or stairwells
PLAN
Two escape routes: primary and secondary, in case one is blocked
PRACTISE
Using your escape routes during fire drills – familiarity saves time
KEY TAKEAWAY

Fire doors save lives – never prop them open or obstruct escape routes.

LESSON 3
The Fire Action Notice

The Fire Action Notice is probably the single most important sign in your building.

📋
FIRE ACTION NOTICE
Located near fire alarm call points and exits
LOCATE
Your Fire Action Notice today
READ
The instructions carefully – each building may differ
UNDERSTAND
Who to contact, where to assemble, any specific duties
FOLLOW
The instructions exactly – don't improvise
KEY TAKEAWAY

Read your Fire Action Notice before an emergency happens – not during one.

Emergency Response

Knowing what to do in a fire emergency can save lives. Remember: if in doubt, get out!

LESSON 4
If You Discover a Fire

Your first priority is to raise the alarm. This gives everyone maximum time to evacuate safely.

1

ACTIVATE

The fire alarm immediately using the nearest call point

2

CALL

The fire brigade (999) or follow your organisation's procedure

3

SWITCH OFF

Any safety-critical machinery if safe to do so

4

CLOSE

The door if leaving a room with fire – slows the spread

5

ONLY TACKLE

The fire if trained and it's blocking your escape

KEY TAKEAWAY

Sound the alarm FIRST – then call emergency services. Never delay raising the alarm.

LESSON 4
When the Fire Alarm Sounds

Your one and only priority is to evacuate. Treat every alarm as real.

LEAVE
Immediately by your primary escape route, escorting any visitors
GO STRAIGHT
To your assembly point and report to the fire warden
DO NOT
Use lifts – they may stop or open onto a floor with fire
DO NOT
Stop for belongings – your life is more important
DO NOT
Return until officially told it is safe
KEY TAKEAWAY

Never use the lift during a fire – always use the stairs.

LESSON 4
If Trapped by Smoke

Over 50% of fire-related deaths are caused by smoke inhalation, not fire itself.

50%+
of fire deaths are caused by smoke inhalation
⬇️

GET DOWN LOW

Smoke rises, cleaner air is near the floor

🧣

COVER YOUR MOUTH

Damp cloth helps filter smoke

🚪

FEEL DOORS

If hot, do not open

⚠️

IF TRAPPED

Seal gaps, go to window to signal

KEY TAKEAWAY

Get low and go – smoke rises, so cleaner air is always near the floor.
