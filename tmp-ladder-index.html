Why Accidents Happen

Most ladder accidents are entirely preventable. Research shows that the majority of incidents are caused by human error rather than equipment failure.

Wrong Ladder Using a ladder unsuitable for the task at hand
Incorrect Setup Poor positioning or unstable base causing instability
Overreaching Leaning too far instead of repositioning the ladder
No Inspection Using damaged or defective equipment without checking
Rushing Taking shortcuts to save time puts you at serious risk
Key Takeaway

The good news is that most ladder accidents are preventable with proper training and by following simple safety procedures.

Pre-Use Inspection

Every ladder must be inspected before each use. A quick visual check takes just seconds and could prevent a serious accident.

Stiles Check side rails for cracks, bends or damage
Rungs Ensure all rungs are secure, straight and intact
Feet Check rubber feet are present and not worn
Locks Test that locking mechanisms engage properly
Labels Safety labels should be readable and intact
Key Takeaway

A 30-second inspection before each use could save your life. Never assume a ladder is safe â always check.

When NOT to Use a Ladder

Ladders are suitable for low-risk, short-duration tasks. There are situations where a ladder is not the right choice.

Duration Tasks requiring more than 30 minutes at height
Two Hands Work that requires both hands (no free hand for holding on)
Heavy Loads Heavy or bulky loads that affect your balance
Weather Adverse weather conditions â wind, rain, or ice
Health When you feel unwell, dizzy or fatigued

Stepladder Setup

Stepladders are self-supporting and do not lean against a wall. They have their own setup requirements to ensure stability.

Fully Open Open the ladder until spreader bars lock into place
Never Partial Never use a stepladder partially open or as a leaning ladder
Firm Ground Place on firm, level ground with all four feet in contact
Face Work Position the ladder facing the work â not side-on
Stable Surface Avoid soft or uneven surfaces that could cause tipping
Key Takeaway

A stepladder must always be fully open with spreader bars locked before you climb. Never take shortcuts with setup.

Securing the Ladder

Wherever possible, a ladder should be secured to prevent it slipping or moving during use.

Tie Tie the top of the ladder to a fixed point where possible
Stabilisers Use stabilisers or stand-offs for additional support
Footing If securing is not possible, have someone foot the ladder
Doors Never place ladders in front of unlocked doors
Walkways Use barriers or signs if the ladder is in a walkway

When footing a ladder, the person should stand facing the ladder with one foot on the bottom rung and hands on the stiles.

Avoiding Overreaching

Overreaching is one of the most common causes of ladder falls. It shifts your centre of gravity and can cause the ladder to tip.

Belt Buckle Keep your belt buckle between the stiles at all times
Reposition If you cannot reach comfortably, climb down and move the ladder
No Leaning Never lean to the side â move the ladder instead
Face Forward Do not work side-on to a stepladder
Same Rung Both feet should remain on the same rung while working
Key Takeaway

The "belt buckle rule" is simple: if your belt buckle moves outside the stiles, you're overreaching. Move the ladder!

Lesson 5

Maintenance, Storage & Transport

As well as pre-use checks, ladders require regular formal inspections, proper maintenance, and correct storage to remain safe.

Maintenance

Clean ladders regularly to remove mud, grease and debris. Lubricate moving parts like locks and pulleys periodically. Check ropes on extension ladders for fraying. Keep records of all formal inspections. Only allow competent persons to carry out repairs.

Storage & Transport

Store on racks that support the full length. Keep wooden ladders away from excessive heat or damp. Keep fibreglass ladders away from direct sunlight. Secure ladders during transport. Never store materials or hang items on ladders.

Key Takeaway

Proper care and storage extends the safe working life of your ladders. A damaged ladder should be immediately taken out of service and labelled.
